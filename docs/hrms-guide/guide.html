1The Big Picture

HRMS is built so that most day-to-day rules are settings, not code. An administrator can change who sees a page, who approves a leave request, which servers an employee can open, and which office WiFi is trusted, all from the browser. Nobody has to edit code or restart anything.

This guide explains each of those controls in simple terms, where to find it, and what happens behind the scenes.

1.1What is configurable

1.2How the pieces fit together

Three questions, three settings

Can you do it? is answered by roles and permissions. Who must approve it? is answered by approval templates. Can you connect from here? is answered by server access and the WiFi whitelist.

2Roles & Permissions (RBAC)

RBAC means Role Based Access Control. Instead of deciding for each person what they can click, you give them a role, and the role carries a bundle of permissions.

2.1The vocabulary

Permission
One single ability, written as module.action, for example leave.view or server.manage.
Role
A named bundle of permissions, for example HR Manager.
User role
The link that gives a person a role. One person can hold more than one.
Direct permission
A single permission given to one person outside any role. It can have an expiry date.
Level
A number from 1 (view only) up to 4 or 5 (admin) that shows how powerful a permission or role is.
Superuser
A built-in super administrator. Passes every check automatically.

2.2How HRMS decides yes or no

Every protected page and button asks the same question. The answer is found in this order, and the first yes wins.

  1. Not logged in? The answer is no.
  2. Superuser? The answer is yes, always.
  3. Does the person have this permission given directly, and has it not expired? Then yes.
  4. Does any of the person's active roles include this permission? Then yes.
  5. Otherwise the answer is no.
Department scope

Passing the permission check is not always enough. A role can be limited to one department or team, so a person may be allowed to view leave but only for their own department's data.

2.3The default roles

These are created automatically when the permission catalog is seeded. They can be edited, and new roles can be added.

RoleTypeLevelIn simple terms
Administrator / Super Adminadmin4Everything, including RBAC and settings
HR Managerhr3People, leave, attendance, recruitment, onboarding, resignation
Managermanager2Team view, attendance marking, DSR and expense approval views
Employeeemployee1Own leave, attendance check-in, own DSR and expenses
Marketing Managermarketing_manager3The full marketing module plus DSR and expenses
Giving someone access

Priya in Accounts needs to see the server list but is not an administrator. Do not make her an admin. Give her the single permission server.view, and nothing else changes for her.

2.4Giving access, step by step

  1. Open Admin > RBAC > User Roles and pick the person.
  2. Add a role, or open that person's permissions and add a single permission. Set an expiry date if the access is temporary.
  3. Role and permission changes are not instant for everyone. They are sent as a request and wait for someone with rbac.approve_assignments.
  4. The approver opens Pending Requests and approves or rejects, one request or a whole batch at a time.
  5. After approval the access is live straight away. The change is written to the audit log.

2.5Building your own role

  1. Admin > RBAC > Roles > Create. Give it a clear name and a type.
  2. Open the role's permissions and tick what it should be able to do.
  3. Assign the role to people as in 2.4.
Never judge by role name

HRMS checks permissions, not role names. Renaming a role never removes access, and a new role works the moment its permissions are ticked. The one exception is the fallback approval chain in Part 4, which looks at the role types hr, manager and ceo.

4Approval Templates

An approval template answers one question: when this person submits something, who must approve it, and in what order. Leave, attendance, expenses, daily reports, marketing and service work orders can each have their own chain.

4.1The three building blocks

Approval template

A named chain, for example Senior Flow. It has a category and optionally a limit on how many levels are needed.

Step

One level in the chain. Level 1 is asked first, then level 2, and so on. Each step names one approver.

Mapping (route)

The rule that says this template applies to this person, this department or this role, for this kind of request.

4.2Which requests can have templates

Template categoryUsed for
Leave ManagementLeave requests
AttendanceAttendance corrections
MarketingMarketing approvals
ExpenseDaily expense vouchers
Daily Service ReportDSR approvals
Service Work OrderWork order approvals

4.3Who gets picked as approver

When someone submits a request, HRMS looks for a mapping in this order and stops at the first one it finds.

  1. A mapping set for that exact employee.
  2. Otherwise a mapping set for the employee's department.
  3. Otherwise a mapping set for the employee's role.
  4. Otherwise the built-in default chain.
A found mapping is final

Once a mapping is found it is used completely. If the template has no step for the next level, the chain simply ends and the request is approved. HRMS does not fall back to the default chain.

4.4The built-in default chain

Used only when no mapping matches.

Who submitsLevel 1Level 2
Regular employeeTheir department managerAn HR user
ManagerThe CEO, or else the first superuserNone

4.5Setting up a chain, step by step

  1. Open Workflows > Approval Templates and choose New.
  2. Give it a name, pick the category, and add the steps. Level 1 first, then level 2, each with an approver.
  3. Optionally set Levels needed. If you configure three steps but set it to two, only two are used.
  4. Save, then open Assign (or Leave > Template Mapping for bulk changes).
  5. Choose who it applies to: employees, a department, or a role.
  6. Check Active Mappings, a read-only page, to confirm who is covered.
Two-level leave approval for Service team

Template Service Leave has level 1 as the Service team lead and level 2 as the HR Manager. Map it to the Service department for Leave. Every leave request from anyone in Service now goes to the team lead first and then to HR. A single employee in Service can still get their own mapping, which wins over the department's.

One template per category per target

A person, department or role cannot be given two templates of the same category at once. Remove the old assignment first.

4.6Who may change templates

Both permissions below are level 4, so by default only administrators.

  • workflow.approval_template_builder opens the builder and mapping pages.
  • workflow.approval_template_edit allows editing and deleting templates.

4.7For other systems

Other apps can read the resolved chain for any user over the API. They can read it but not change it.

5Servers

HRMS keeps a directory of servers (RDP by default) and a list of which employees may connect to each one. The TunnelGate app asks HRMS which servers a person is allowed to see.

Addresses only, never passwords

HRMS stores the server's address and nothing else. People sign in on the server itself. There is nowhere in HRMS to type a server password.

5.1Adding a server

  1. Open Admin > Servers and choose Add. This needs server.manage.
  2. Enter a display name such as Accounts RDP.
  3. Enter the host: a name or IP only, for example rdp-128.example.com or 192.168.1.102. No port, no http://, no spaces. It is stored in lower case and must be unique.
  4. Choose the protocol (RDP, SSH, VNC or Other) and add a short description.
  5. Save. The server is active straight away.

5.2Choosing who can connect

  1. Open the server and choose Access. This needs server.assign_access.
  2. Tick the employees who may use it and save.
PersonWhat they see through the API
Employee with a grantOnly the servers they were granted
SuperuserEvery active server
Holder of server.access_allEvery active server, with no grants needed
Employee with no grantNothing

5.3Switching off and removing

  • Deactivate: hides the server from the API but keeps its access list. Reactivate later and everyone is back.
  • Delete: removes the server and all its access rows for good.
  • Access is per person. Department or role based access is not supported.

5.4Server permissions at a glance

CodeLevelMeaning
server.view2See the list and use the look-up-another-user API
server.manage3Add, edit, activate, deactivate, delete
server.assign_access3Choose who can use each server
server.access_all4Unrestricted access to every server
Nobody has these by default

The server permissions are not attached to any default role. Until someone assigns them, only superusers can manage servers.

6WiFi Access Control

This feature lets administrators decide which office WiFi networks are trusted. HRMS keeps the list and answers one question: is this network allowed? It does not watch or block any traffic itself. The TunnelGate app detects the user's WiFi and asks HRMS before granting access.

6.1Adding a WiFi network

  1. Open Settings > WiFi Access Control. This needs settings.wifi_networks.
  2. Choose Add Network.
  3. Name: a label for people, such as Head Office or Branch 2.
  4. SSID: the exact WiFi name people see on their device.
  5. BSSID, optional: the access point's MAC address, written AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF. HRMS checks the format and converts it to capitals.
  6. Leave Active ticked and save.

6.2SSID only, or pinned to one access point

SetupHow it matchesWhen to use it
SSID only (BSSID left blank)Any router broadcasting that name is acceptedSimple setups where you trust the name
SSID with BSSIDBoth the name and the exact router must matchStops a fake hotspot using your WiFi name
Add as many as you need

Head office, each branch and a guest network can all be listed. The first match wins. Switching a network to inactive removes it from checks without deleting it.

6.3The on and off switch

At the top of the same page is Enforce WiFi Restriction for Server Access. It is off by default.

SwitchWhat HRMS answers
OffAlways allowed. No matching is done at all. Safe for testing TunnelGate first.
On, with networks listedAllowed only if the current network matches one entry
On, with no networks listedDenied, with a message to contact the administrator

6.4Exempting a person

A person holding settings.wifi_bypass skips the WiFi check entirely. Superusers are exempt too. Use this sparingly, for example for people who must connect from home.

6.5How TunnelGate checks

  • TunnelGate calls POST /api/rbac/wifi-networks/validate/ with the ssid and optional bssid.
  • If HRMS cannot be reached, TunnelGate should deny. Failing open defeats the purpose.
  • TunnelGate should remember an allow decision for 30 to 60 seconds and check again as soon as the network changes.
  • Use a dedicated service account for TunnelGate, never a real employee's login.

7Other Things You Can Change

Smaller settings that follow the same idea: change it in the browser, no code needed.

Geofence locations

Add plant or office locations with a map point and an allowed range. Attendance check-ins are matched against them. Needs settings.geofencing.

Company settings

Company profile, logo and shared dropdown values used across forms. Needs settings.manage_company.

Email settings and templates

Outgoing email account and the wording of system emails. Needs settings.manage_email and settings.manage_templates.

Departments and designations

The organisation structure that approval mappings and scopes refer to. Needs org.manage_departments and org.manage_designations.

Holidays and weekly offs

Holiday calendar by year and weekly off pattern. Needs the holiday.manage permissions.

Intranet apps

Tiles in the app launcher with name, link, icon, colour and order. Can be switched on or off. Needs intranet.manage.

Forms and workflows

Custom forms and multi-step workflows built in the browser. Needs forms.* and workflow.* permissions.

Onboarding policies

The policies new joiners see in the public onboarding form. Needs onboarding_policy.*.

8Quick Checklists

8.1A new person joins

  1. Create the employee. The login and the basic Employee role are created automatically.
  2. Check Active Mappings that their department or role has the right approval templates.
  3. Give extra roles or single permissions only if needed, then have them approved.
  4. Grant server access and, if needed, note any WiFi exemption.

8.2A person leaves or changes role

  1. Remove or replace their role in User Roles. Remove any direct permissions.
  2. Remove their server access grants.
  3. If they are named as an approver in any template, replace them there first. Otherwise requests may reach someone who has left.

8.3Things worth remembering

  • Everyone's access is decided by permissions, so adjust permissions rather than creating special cases.
  • A role or permission change waits for approval and then applies immediately.
  • An approval mapping always beats the default chain, even if the template is short.
  • Servers are by person, WiFi is by network, and the two are independent switches.
  • Every change is recorded in the audit log, so you can always see who changed what.
